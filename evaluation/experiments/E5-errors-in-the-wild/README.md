# E5 — errors in published health metadata

Spec: `~/dev/kanzo/papers/swat4hcls2027/EVIDENCE.md`, §E5.

**Claim it supports:** post-hoc validation is failing in practice; entry-time
validation would have prevented much of it.

## Status

**Complete and verified. Re-run 2 Sep 2026 on one engine.** The corpus is
validated by rudof — `@kanzo-tech/rudof-wasm@0.3.8`, the engine this library
ships and the engine the form runs on commit — and by nothing else.

The headline is **69.1%** of records violating the profile they claim, and
**96.3%** of the defects found structurally preventable at entry. Both numbers
moved, slightly, and both moves are measured rather than argued. See
"What moved, and why" below.

Numbers live in [`results/wild-errors.md`](results/wild-errors.md), generated by
`report.py` from `results/summary.json`. Nothing in that file is hand-typed, and
nothing in *this* file is either — every figure below is copied from a generated
artefact, which supersedes this README if they ever disagree.

## One engine, and why the second one is gone

The engine is `@kanzo-tech/rudof-wasm@0.3.8`, instantiated headlessly and driven
through the same `Session` ABI that `src/engine/RudofEngine.ts` programs
against. No test double, no fork checkout. rdflib 7.6.0 is still in the
pipeline and is not an exception to "one engine": it parses, cleans and
re-serializes the harvested Turtle, and decides nothing about conformance.

An earlier round of this experiment validated the whole corpus twice — once with
rudof and once with pySHACL — and diffed the two reports defect by defect. **That
instrument earned its keep.** The two engines agreed on every defect and every
record, but the 102 results they disagreed about outside the counted buckets were
two real rudof defects:

1. **`sh:targetClass` did not select through `rdfs:subClassOf`.** SHACL §1.1
   makes the SHACL types of a term include the SHACL superclasses of its
   `rdf:type` values, and §2.1.3.2 makes the SHACL instances of a class the
   target. A node typed `dcat:Role`, with `dcat:Role rdfs:subClassOf
   skos:Concept` in the data graph, is a target of `sh:targetClass skos:Concept`.
   rudof did not select it, at one hop or two.
2. **The Turtle parser silently discarded triples whose IRI it would not
   accept.** `loadData` returned, nothing was raised, and the triple was simply
   absent from the graph. Rejecting such an IRI is defensible and accepting it is
   defensible; discarding it and then reporting `conforms: true` is the one
   behaviour that is not, because it means a record can be made to pass by
   embedding a malformed IRI in it.

**Both are fixed and published in 0.3.8.** So the instrument has done its job and
comes out of the published claims: rudof is the product's engine, and a second
SHACL implementation is a workshop instrument, never evidence. Two things
replaced it, and neither needs a second engine:

- [`conformance.py`](conformance.py) probes every SHACL construct these two
  shapes graphs use against a minimal case whose correct answer is forced by the
  specification's own TEXTUAL DEFINITION — `expect` is the spec's answer, never
  another engine's. It carries the minimal reproducer for each of the two bugs
  above. **rudof 0.3.8 scores 30/30 on the constraint cases and 4/4 on the
  parser cases**, against 28/30 and 1/4 for the version that produced the
  original run. Report:
  [`results/rudof-conformance.md`](results/rudof-conformance.md).
- **The parse is accounted for on every run.** `validate.py` compares the triple
  count of the file it staged against the quad count rudof loaded from it. Zero
  difference, or a raised error, are the two acceptable answers. This is the
  check that would have caught bug 2, and it never needed a second SHACL engine
  — only a count. Over the 370 records rudof read: **93,395 triples staged,
  93,395 loaded, 0 lost.**

## What moved, and why

Measured, not asserted, by
[`version_delta.py`](version_delta.py) → [`results/version-delta.md`](results/version-delta.md):
the **identical** 372 staged validation graphs are re-run through the previous
release, so every difference is a difference between the two engine builds.

| | 0.3.5 (the original run) | 0.3.8 (now) |
|---|---:|---:|
| records the parser refused | 0 | **2** |
| records flagged (L1 + L2) | 259 | 257 |
| defects | 1,321 | 1,317 |

**The parser fix accounts for the entire movement.** The two records 0.3.8
refuses carried exactly 4 defects under 0.3.5 — one `sh:datatype`, three
`sh:in` — which is exactly the drop in the defect total, and the two records
that stopped being flagged are exactly the two refused ones. Nothing else in the
corpus changed.

**The target-selection fix changed no defect at all.** Restricted to the 370
records both versions read, the defect count is 1,317 under both. The fix is not
invisible — it produces **+101 `sh:minCount` results** — but all 101 land in the
`scaffolding` bucket, which is where results about EU authority concepts go and
which never reaches a defect count. That is exactly what the retired cross-check
predicted: the 102 results pySHACL used to emit and rudof did not "all land in
buckets this experiment discards, which is why they do not move the headline".
It is now a measurement instead of a prediction.

**A record the parser refuses is not a clean record.** Counting one as
conformant would be the same silent pass that bug 2 used to produce, so the two
refused records carry an L3 defect, `UnparseableRecord`. L3 is exactly "a defect
no SHACL profile can express" — there is no shape that catches an IRI the
grammar excludes, because the triple never reaches the graph. That is why they
sit in the *not preventable* bucket, and it is worth being precise about what
that bucket means here: these two **are** preventable at entry, by a form that
checks IRI well-formedness before writing. Ours does not yet do that (see the
form backlog). The bucket is a statement about SHACL, not about forms.

So the honest summary of the headline movement is: **69.6% → 69.1% and
96.5% → 96.3%, both because two records in the sample carry a malformed IRI and
the engine now says so instead of quietly dropping the triple.** The old figures
are not wrong about the corpus; they were produced by an engine that could not
read 607 triples of it and did not say so.

### Preventability, re-measured

From `results/preventability.json`, joined against what the form *actually*
renders (`results/form-fields.json`, produced by `form_fields.harness.ts` from
the real `buildFormModel` and the real widget registry).

| Over all 1,367 defects | defects | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Prevented by the **widget** — the bad value is untypable | **414** | **30.3%** |
| Prevented by **validation** on commit | 903 | 66.1% |
| **Not prevented** | 50 | 3.7% |
| Prevented, either way | 1,317 | **96.3%** |

Against DCAT-AP 3.0.1 **as SEMIC publishes it** (no `sh:in`, no `sh:pattern`,
so a form generated from it has neither the control nor the check): 37.1% /
13.2% / 49.7%.

**The widget column used to be 0.0% and is not any more.** The reason is a
change in the library, not in the corpus: `buildFormModel` now merges property
shapes that share a focus and a path into one field whose constraints are the
conjunction (`src/form/conjunction.ts`). That was the top item on this
experiment's own form backlog, and closing it did what the backlog said it
would. Measured on the same shapes: the published DCAT-AP inventory falls from
302 fields over 131 distinct (class, path) pairs — 118 of them carrying more
than one field, up to 4 on one predicate — to **131 fields over 131 pairs, 0
duplicated**. One control per predicate, carrying every constraint on it.

What the widget now prevents, and why:

| | defects | why |
|---|---:|---|
| `sh:datatype` | 330 | every control on the path stamps the declared datatype |
| `sh:maxCount` | 45 | every control on the path is capped, so "+ Add" stops |
| `sh:nodeKind` | 39 | every control on the path emits an IRI by construction |

What it still does not prevent, with the **new** reason in each case — these are
not the old reasons restated:

- **594 `sh:in`, the largest single class.** The merge fuses property shapes
  *within* a node shape. L2's enumerations are a separate node shape that this
  study wrote, on the same class and path as the published profile's open
  control, so there are still 2 fields on that path and 1 of them accepts free
  input. This is a fact about validating a published profile against
  requirements it does not encode, and it would not arise for a profile that
  stated its own `sh:in`.
- **136 `sh:class`.** The reference control sets `allowCustomValue` on purpose,
  so any IRI can be typed. Deliberate, and it defeats prevention by construction.
- **97 `sh:nodeKind`.** `primitiveToTerm` emits an IRI only for `sh:nodeKind
  sh:IRI`; DCAT-AP says `sh:BlankNodeOrIRI` on every property this corpus fails,
  so the binding layer writes a literal. Unchanged by the merge and still the
  second form-backlog item.
- **40 `sh:minCount`.** The array control never blocks removing the last value;
  `sh:minCount` is presentational in the UI and enforced only by the validator.
- **18 `sh:minLength` and 17 `sh:pattern`.** Both reach the input as HTML
  attributes that never fire: `minlength` fires on submit and the form calls
  `preventDefault` on submit; `sh:pattern` is deliberately not passed to the HTML
  `pattern` attribute, because an unanchored XPath regex is not an anchored HTML
  one.

**And the gate is still the host application's to close.** `useMetadataForm` runs
`RudofEngine.validateTree`, which compiles the whole shapes graph and validates
every node of the projected tree; all 1,317 carry severity `sh:Violation` and so
drop `form.isValid` to false. But `MetadataForm`'s `<form>` calls
`preventDefault`, there is no submit button, and `toTurtle()` serializes
unconditionally. "Prevented by validation" means the form surfaces the violation
and offers the gate, not that the library closes it.

## Reproducibility

Every number in the writeup is regenerated by a committed script. Verified this
pass:

- `validate.py` re-run from the committed raw corpus reproduces `summary.json`
  and `violations.csv` **byte-identically** (modulo the `generated_utc` stamp),
  and `report.py` reproduces `wild-errors.md`.
- `results/rudof-conformance.json` and `results/version-delta.json` are
  byte-stable across runs too. `results/preventability.json` is stable for a
  given `results/form-fields.json`; it moved this pass because the form changed,
  which is the point of measuring it against the form rather than asserting it.
- `data/manifest.csv` verifies: 372 records, 0 missing, 0 checksum mismatches.
- The vendored DCAT-AP shapes match the SHA-256 in
  `data/dcat-ap-3.0.1/PROVENANCE.md`.
- Row ordering is total everywhere it needs to be. rudof iterates a node shape's
  property shapes in hash order, and this profile puts up to four property
  shapes on one path, so a sort by (shape, path) leaves real ties. Sorting on
  the full row removed the churn; the multiset was never affected and no
  reported number moved.
- The **only** fields that vary between runs on unchanged inputs are the
  `generated_utc` stamps.

**The harvest is not re-run as part of verification, and cannot be.** It is
network-bound and deliberately rate-limited (one request at a time, 0.7 s apart;
`linkcheck.py` enforces 20 s per third-party host), and re-harvesting on a later
date would not reproduce the corpus anyway — the records change under us. The
manifest's SHA-256 column is what tells you whether a re-harvest matched. The
derived stages are cheap: `validate.py` is ~7 s over all 372 records, staging
included, which is a fifth of what the two-engine run cost.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — the shipped SHACL is a fraction of the profile

DCAT-AP 3.0.1's `dcat-ap-SHACL.ttl` contains **zero** `sh:in` and **zero**
`sh:pattern`. The same document's prose carries a table headed *"controlled
vocabularies that MUST be used"* and says of that class of requirement
*"Validation systems SHOULD produce errors."* No validator running the published
shapes can produce those errors, because the published shapes do not encode them.

That is the gap between the L1 and the L1+L2 numbers — 37.4% against 69.1% — and
it is the strongest form of the paper's argument: post-hoc validation is not
merely being skipped, in this ecosystem it is **structurally unable** to check
what the profile requires.

Two more artefact-level defects found while building L1, both worth reporting to
SEMIC independently of the paper:

- `dcat:DataServiceShape` has two `sh:property` links pointing at IRIs with no
  triples anywhere in the file, so it states two constraints that say nothing. A
  profile that cannot round-trip through its own document is a profile nobody
  validated. They are dropped before validating; an empty property shape
  constrains nothing, so the drop cannot hide a violation — and `conformance.py`
  checks that rather than asserting it, by validating a deliberately-violating
  synthetic `dcat:DataService` under both the published and the de-dangled
  shapes and requiring the same four results.
- `sh:class` on `dct:license`, `dct:format`, `dcat:landingPage` and
  `spdx:algorithm` cannot be satisfied by an ordinary web URL, because nobody
  publishes an `rdf:type` for a Creative Commons deed or an IANA media-type page.
  Every record raises these results, every run: **46.2%** of everything the
  validator hands an operator is this. A report that is nearly half unfixable
  noise is a report nobody reads twice — which is the practical mechanism behind
  "post-hoc validation is failing".

### Finding 2 — the defects are ungated vocabulary drift, not carelessness

All figures from `summary.json → value_shapes`.

- **`adms:status`: not one value in the sample uses the authority table DCAT-AP
  3.0.1 mandates.** 187 of 195 are on `purl.org/adms/status`, the vocabulary the
  earlier revision specified; the other 8 are on a neighbouring EU table. This is
  a vocabulary migration that nothing forced anybody through. No gate ever said
  "that table moved".
- **`dcat:byteSize`: 215 defects, all datatype.** The profile requires
  `xsd:nonNegativeInteger`; 46% of values are an untyped literal and 43% a plain
  `xsd:integer`. Only 11% are right. Nobody catches that by eye — and a form
  generated from the shape emits the declared datatype by construction, which is
  now measured: all 330 `sh:datatype` defects fall in the widget-prevented
  column.
- **`dct:format`: 340 defects.** 22% of values are an IRI from no mandated
  registry at all and 5.5% are a bare literal (`CSV`, `XLSX`, `application/json`)
  where an IRI is required. Swapped-with-`dcat:mediaType` values: 7. The mistake
  is not confusion between two vocabularies; it is the absence of any mechanism
  that asks a vocabulary for a value.

None of these is an error any shipped DCAT-AP validator will report.

### Finding 3 — the ecosystem already repairs downstream

**38,821** literals in the sample — 297 of 372 records — are machine translations
the aggregator produced after the fact, tagged `-t-…-t0-mt…`. Meanwhile 15.9% of
records carry a `dct:title` with no language tag at all. Post-hoc translation of
a monolingual record is the same move as post-hoc validation of an invalid one:
repair downstream, because nothing upstream asked. Feeds §5 and E3.

### Finding 4 — four harness bugs that changed the answer

Recorded because they are the methodological warning this experiment carries.

1. The first harvest used `DESCRIBE`. This Virtuoso returns a blank node's
   identity but not its properties, so every record appeared to have an empty
   `dcat:contactPoint`, `dct:provenance` and `adms:identifier`. That version of
   the experiment reported a *much* worse and entirely wrong number.
2. `sh:class` resolves `rdfs:subClassOf*` **in the data graph**, so the subclass
   axioms have to be loaded or every correctly-typed `vcard:Individual` contact
   point fails an `sh:class vcard:Kind` it satisfies. That is
   `data/class-hierarchy.ttl`'s whole job.
3. The README once asserted a headline the pipeline did not produce, and a
   finding the corpus did not support, for a full day — because both were
   hand-typed rather than generated. The rule that every number must come from a
   committed script exists for exactly this, and it only works if it is applied
   to the prose as well as to the tables.
4. **The engine's parser was silently dropping input**, and for one full run the
   experiment could not have known. The check that catches it is a triple count,
   not a second engine. It is now part of every run.

Any "N% of records are broken" figure produced without the first two fixes is
measuring its own harness. Worth a sentence in §8.

### Finding 5 — rudof against the specification, not against another engine

Full detail in [`results/rudof-conformance.md`](results/rudof-conformance.md).

Agreement between two implementations is not correctness; both can be wrong the
same way, and the corpus can only report what these 372 records happen to
exercise. So every SHACL construct the two shapes graphs use is probed in
isolation, with the expected answer taken from the Recommendation's own textual
definition.

**rudof 0.3.8: 30/30 constraint cases, 4/4 parser cases.** The version that
produced the original run scored 28/30 and 1/4; the five cases it failed are the
minimal reproducers for the two bugs named above, and all five pass now. rudof
is right about `sh:targetClass` including the subclass closure, `sh:class` with
subclasses, `sh:datatype` including ill-typed literals, `sh:nodeKind`,
`sh:minCount`, `sh:maxCount`, `sh:pattern` including unanchored matching and
`sh:flags`, `sh:minLength`, `sh:uniqueLang` including the untagged case,
`sh:in`, `sh:closed`, and about refusing an IRI it cannot accept instead of
dropping the triple.

**One place the profile is worse than the engine.** `dcat:DataServiceShape`'s two
dangling `sh:property` links (Finding 1) make no difference to rudof: the same
deliberately-violating `dcat:DataService` yields the same four results —
`Class@dcat:theme`, `MinCount@dcat:endpointURL`, `MinCount@dct:title`,
`NodeKind@dcat:theme` — under the published shapes and under the de-dangled
control. So rudof is not silently skipping the shape; it ignores the two empty
property shapes, which constrain nothing, and validates the rest exactly as a
working profile would be validated. The probe uses a violating record on
purpose: with a conforming one, 0 results is the right answer whether the shape
was validated or skipped.

### Finding 6 — preventability, and what closing a backlog item did to it

Full detail in [`results/preventability.md`](results/preventability.md).

`validate.py` used to assert that L1 and L2 defects are preventable at entry *by
construction*. That was reasoning, not measurement, and when it was first
measured against the form the library actually renders, prevention by the
*control* came out at **0 of 1,369** — because DCAT-AP 3.0.1's generated SHACL
puts every constraint in its own property shape, and `buildFormModel` rendered
one open field per shape. A closed enumeration standing beside three open text
boxes on the same predicate does not make anything untypable.

That was the top item on this experiment's form backlog. It is now closed
(`src/form/conjunction.ts` merges property shapes sharing a focus and a path
into one field carrying the conjunction of their constraints), and the number
moved accordingly: **prevention by the widget is 30.3%, not 0%** — 414 of 1,367
defects, carried by `sh:datatype` (330), `sh:maxCount` (45) and `sh:nodeKind`
(39). The published-profile inventory falls from 302 fields over 131 distinct
(class, path) pairs to 131 fields over 131 pairs, with **no** pair carrying more
than one field.

The remaining 66.1% are caught by commit-time validation rather than made
untypable, and the reasons are now specific and individually actionable rather
than one structural excuse — see the table in "Preventability, re-measured"
above. The two that are still ours to fix are `primitiveToTerm` treating only
`sh:nodeKind sh:IRI` as IRI-producing (97 defects) and `sh:minCount` not
blocking removal of the last array value (40).

**Two things E1's "cannot render" list predicted, that do not materialise here.**
`sh:or` and complex paths rendering read-only contribute nothing, because these
shapes use neither: **302 `sh:path` values, every one a plain predicate IRI** —
0 complex paths, 0 `sh:node`, 0 `sh:or`, 0 `sh:hasValue`, 0 `sh:minExclusive`.
The measured field inventory follows: 0 read-only, 0 nested sub-forms.
`form_fields.harness.ts` asserts `complexPath === 0` and `readOnly === 0`, so if
a future revision of these shapes introduces one, the run fails and the buckets
get revisited rather than quietly going stale. That is a fact about this profile
and not a clean bill of health for the form.

**And one caveat the numbers do not carry.** DCAT-AP declares no `sh:node` at
all, so a form rooted at the Dataset shape projects a tree of exactly one node
and `validateTree` never reaches the distribution. The figures above take the
charitable reading, that each class is edited under its own shape. Under the
one-form reading, **1,171 defects (85.7%)** sit on a class other than the root
and would be neither rendered nor validated.

The honest sentence for the paper is therefore: **96.3% of the defects are
prevented at entry — 30.3% because the generated control cannot express the bad
value, and 66.1% because the form validates the whole shapes graph on commit and
drops `form.isValid`, a gate the host application must choose to close.**

## What weakens the claim

Full list in `results/wild-errors.md` under "threats to validity". Ranked by what
a reviewer will reach for first:

1. **One engine.** This is the claim that got weaker when pySHACL came out. The
   corpus is now validated by the engine we wrote, and "we tuned the checker to
   the claim" no longer has a second implementation standing against it. Three
   things are offered in its place and none of them is the same thing: the
   engine is probed construct by construct against the specification's own words
   rather than against another engine (30/30, 4/4); the parse is accounted for
   on every run, so the engine cannot report conformance about input it did not
   read; and `version_delta.py` shows what the two most recent engine fixes did
   to the corpus, which is the closest available thing to an independent
   re-analysis. What is genuinely gone is the ability to say "an implementation
   we did not write agrees". The paper should not claim it.
2. **The aggregator is in the path.** These are records as a *consumer* receives
   them, not as a publisher wrote them. The aggregator harvests, normalises and
   re-serialises, so some defects may be introduced or repaired in that step. No
   defect can be attributed to any particular actor — which is also why none is.
   The claim survives, because the argument is that *no* stage of the chain has
   an entry-time gate.
3. **This is not a uniform random sample, and 372 of 33,228 is a 1.12% fraction.**
   The floor of 2 and cap of 40 make a record's chance of being drawn vary by a
   factor of **191** between strata — 0.52% in the largest catalogue, 100% in the
   17 catalogues small enough that the floor took all of them. 37 strata sit at
   the floor, 1 at the cap. The weighted design effect is 1.43, so the sample
   carries about as much information as **260** records drawn uniformly. The
   design supports an ecosystem-level statement (69.1% sample, 78.7% weighted,
   both with intervals well clear of any threshold that would make the claim
   marginal). It does **not** support any statement about a single catalogue: at
   the floor a stratum contributes 2 records. That is a limitation and also the
   point — the design cannot produce a league table even by accident.
4. **The preventable share is inventory-dependent.** Everything SHACL can
   express is *checkable*, so the second headline is only as meaningful as the L3
   checks in the denominator. Four are implemented (dead link, stale against
   declared frequency, placeholder value, unparseable record). More would lower
   it. The 81.7% floor is the honest way to quote the size.
5. **The preventability split is a measurement of one version of our own form.**
   It moved from 0% to 30.3% prevented-by-widget this pass because the library
   changed, and it will move again. It is regenerated from
   `results/form-fields.json`, which is produced from the real `buildFormModel`,
   so it cannot go stale silently — but any figure quoted from it needs the
   library version beside it.
6. **L2 is our construction.** DCAT-AP ships no `sh:in`, so authority-table
   conformance had to be written as shapes for this study. It is confined to the
   properties the specification's own "MUST be used" table names; nothing weaker
   (RECOMMENDED, MAY) is scored, and `dcat:theme` is excluded as circular. Two L2
   checks are namespace patterns rather than enumerations, so L2 *under*-counts
   there. The two `sh:minLength` constraints are ours outright.
7. **L1 is a moving target.** DCAT-AP 3.0.1 shipped in June 2026; most sampled
   records predate it and were authored against 2.1.1 or 3.0.0.
8. **L3 is not stable.** Link status was probed once. Re-running on another day
   gives a slightly different count. Only `gone`, `dns_fail` and `conn_fail` are
   counted as dead (13 records); 403/405/429 and TLS failures are recorded and
   deliberately *not* counted.
9. **Theme HEAL is an aggregator classification.** Some records under it are
   health-adjacent rather than health data, and health datasets whose publisher
   did not tag them HEAL are outside the frame.

.venv/bin/python ethics_check.py     # the binding rule, as a gate

The venv is rdflib only. There is no SHACL engine in it: the engine is
`@kanzo-tech/rudof-wasm` from the repository's own `node_modules`, and
`version_delta.py` fetches the one previous release it compares against into a
work directory (`E5_WORK`, default a temp dir).

`validate.py` writes `data/stage/` — the staged validation graphs, `keys.json`,
`meta.json` and rudof's raw report — and must run before `conformance.py`,
`version_delta.py` and `preventability.py`, all three of which read it. None of
them re-validates the corpus: they read the run that produced the published
numbers, so what they bucket is the same set of objects the headline counted
rather than a re-derivation that has to be argued equal.

The vitest harness must run before `preventability.py`, which reads its output.

The derived stages run in well under a minute from an existing `data/raw/`. The
first four are network-bound and polite by construction: `harvest.py` is
single-threaded, one request at a time 0.7 s apart, with a descriptive
User-Agent, and touches nothing that `https://data.europa.eu/robots.txt`
disallows — everything goes through the public SPARQL endpoint. `linkcheck.py`
sends at most one `HEAD` per record and enforces a 20 s minimum interval per
third-party host.

Re-harvesting on a later date will not reproduce the byte-identical corpus — the
records change. `data/manifest.csv` is what tells you whether it did.

## Method in one paragraph

Stratified sample of the 33,228 `HEAL`-themed dataset records on data.europa.eu,
77 strata (one per source catalogue), near-proportional allocation with a floor
of 2 and a cap of 40, fixed seed, uniform draw without replacement inside each
stratum. Each record is fetched as an anchored `CONSTRUCT` closure, cleaned of
aggregator artefacts (`dqv` quality annotations, machine-translated literals),
merged with the `rdfs:subClassOf` axioms and the `rdf:type` of the external IRIs
it references, serialized once, and validated by **rudof
(`@kanzo-tech/rudof-wasm@0.3.8`, wasm)** against three layers: L1 the published
DCAT-AP 3.0.1 SHACL, L2 the controlled-vocabulary requirements the same
specification states in prose but does not express in SHACL, L3 four defect
classes no SHACL profile can express. L1 and L2 are *expressible in SHACL* and
L3 is not; that, and only that, is what the "preventable" bucket in
`summary.json` means. `preventability.py` measures what the form does with them
and reports three numbers instead of one.
